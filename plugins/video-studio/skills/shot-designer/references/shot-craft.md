# Shot craft

## Say it, show it

The strongest explainer pictures show the exact thing being said at the moment it is said:

- The spoken keyword appears on screen with the word (kinetic keywords).
- A callout or highlight points at what the narration names.
- A number is shown as a number that counts up, not a sentence.
- A process is a list that builds one item per spoken step.
- An interface is a simplified, enlarged UI card; label illustrative UI as illustrative and use real captures (image mode) when the claim depends on the real product.

## Rhythm

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Something visible changes every 2–5 s | Viewers read stillness as "nothing new" and swipe |
| No shot shorter than ~0.8 s | Text needs time to be read |
| Vary layouts; avoid 4 of the same mode in a row | Repetition reads as a slideshow |
| Cut on the word, not after it | The picture should arrive with the meaning |
| Hold the ending | The CTA needs a beat to land; default 0.8 s after the last word |

## Transitions

- `cut`: default; invisible when motivated.
- `wipe`: a section change (new step, new beat). Graphic wipes also hide jumps between layouts.
- `push`: moving forward through a sequence.
- `fade`: endings, or a tonal shift. Avoid mid-video fades; they feel like the video is ending.

## Contrast and depth

Follow the direction lock's contrast rule (for example, dark surface with one bright accent; warm environment with a cool subject). Use the accent only for what matters on screen right now — the keyword, the active step, the number.

Decide what sits in front and what sits behind. Large type behind a cut-out subject needs a subject matte (segmentation); without one, keep type beside or below the subject rather than faking occlusion.

## Camera shots (footage recipes)

When a shot is `camera`, the prompt-free fields must still be concrete:

- `framing`: wide full-body, medium shot, MCU, close-up, over-the-shoulder, profile.
- `movement`: a direction and a reason — "slow push in toward the screen as she points at it", or "static, locked-off".
- `action`: what the character does with hands, eyes and body.
- `light_source`: a visible, motivated source (window left, desk lamp warm, monitor glow).
- `sound`: what we hear in the shot.

"Cinematic" is not an instruction. Movement without a reason reads as noise; every move should follow attention, reveal information or show cause and effect.
