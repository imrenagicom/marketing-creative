# Direction craft

What each field of an option must contain, and the reasoning behind it.

## Logline

One sentence: who it's for, what changes for them, how the video shows it. If you cannot write it, the idea is not ready — better images will not rescue a weak idea.

## Hook

`pattern` names the mechanism (see [hook-patterns.md](hook-patterns.md)); `opening` describes the first two seconds as picture and sound, concretely: "Three chat bubbles slide in and pile up while the narrator asks…", not "an engaging intro". The hook must be paid off later in the video; a hook the video never delivers on reads as bait.

## Structure

Name the shape and why it fits: problem → promise → numbered steps → payoff → CTA; myth → truth; before → after; list of reasons; question → answer reveal. Explainers retain viewers when the promise is specific ("three steps") and each step delivers visibly.

## Beats

Each beat has one job (`purpose`: hook, promise, step, proof, reveal, payoff, CTA) and a `summary` of what is said and shown. Give rough `seconds` that add up to the target length. Plan escalation: the strongest proof or the reveal comes late; the CTA matches the value delivered ("save this" for reference content, "try it" for a product).

## Characters and acting baselines

Every voice is a character, including an off-screen narrator. The acting baseline is the default performance every later line must follow unless a line says otherwise. Write it as performance, not adjectives:

- energy and pace ("quick, leans on numbers, slows down for the payoff")
- attitude toward the viewer ("a friend who already solved this", never "a salesperson")
- how they speak and move ("short sentences, smiles through the question, points at what she names")
- register and language habits ("Indonesian casual *gua/lo*, English product terms kept in English")

Without a baseline, generated voice and motion default to flat, generic delivery.

## Visual plan

How the picture carries the argument:

- **Show what is said, when it is said.** One keyword per spoken phrase lands on screen with the word; a callout points at whatever the narration names.
- **Simplify interfaces.** A clean, enlarged UI card beats an unreadable screen recording. Mark illustrative UI as illustrative.
- **Change something every 2–5 seconds.** A new keyword, card, layout or camera setup counts; a static frame longer than ~7 s feels stalled.
- **Contrast rule.** Pick the one relationship that keeps the subject readable, such as warm environment vs cool accent, or dark surface with one bright accent.
- **Depth.** Decide what sits behind the subject and what sits in front (text behind a cut-out subject needs segmentation).
- **Motivated motion.** Every move has a reason: reveal the next item, follow attention, emphasise a number. Movement without a reason reads as noise.

## Audio policy

Decide voice (who, model, language later), music (none, low bed, or rhythmic driver) and sound effects (none, UI ticks, transitions). "None" is a valid, explicit choice. Narration intelligibility wins every conflict.

## Risks

Say where the option could fail: a hook that depends on real comments you don't have, a claim that needs proof, a visual that needs footage the project lacks. Honest risks are part of a good proposal.
