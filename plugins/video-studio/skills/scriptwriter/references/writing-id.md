# Writing Indonesian narration

Write in Indonesian from the start. A translated English script usually sounds stiff, runs long and misses the register.

## Register

| `register` | Pronouns | Sounds like | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `casual` | *gua/gue, lo/lu* | "Masih sering lupa ide bagus yang nyelip di chat?" | Jakarta creator-style social, young urban audience |
| `friendly` | *aku, kamu* | "Kamu pasti pernah lupa ide bagus, kan?" | Broad consumer audience, warm brand voice |
| `formal` | *saya, Anda* | "Panduan ini menjelaskan cara mengatur akses tim Anda." | Enterprise, government, training |

Keep one register per speaker for the whole video; mixing *gua* and *Anda* sounds like two people.

## Lines that work out loud

- Use spoken particles sparingly and in the right register: *kan*, *sih*, *dong*, *nih*, *ya* belong to casual and friendly speech, not formal.
- Signpost steps: *Pertama, … Kedua, … Terakhir, …* (casual can use *Satu, … Dua, …*).
- Indonesian words are longer; expect fewer words per second than English. Cut rather than speed up.
- Prefer active, everyday verbs (*simpan*, *coba*, *cek*) over formal nominalisations (*penyimpanan*, *pengecekan*) unless the register is formal.

## English terms (code-switching)

Keep product names, UI labels and tech terms in English when the audience says them in English (*export*, *template*, *dashboard*, *prompt*). Mark them for voice-director so pronunciation is checked: English words inside Indonesian sentences are a common mispronunciation point. Do not mix English grammar into the sentence; only the terms.

## Keywords

On-screen keywords follow the spoken words exactly, including English terms as spoken ("*export*", not "*ekspor*", if that is what the narrator says).

## CTA patterns

- casual: "Save video ini biar nggak lupa." / "Follow buat part dua."
- friendly: "Simpan video ini, terus coba seminggu ya."
- formal: "Simpan panduan ini untuk referensi tim Anda."

## Numbers

Write numbers as they should be spoken when it affects meaning (*dua ribu dua puluh enam*, *lima puluh persen*), and keep on-screen keywords as the viewer expects to read them.
