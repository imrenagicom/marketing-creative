# Writing English narration

## Register

| `register` | Sounds like | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `casual` | "Okay, real talk — this setting is hiding your best feature." | Creator-style social |
| `conversational` | "Here's a three-step habit that actually sticks." | Most explainers |
| `formal` | "This guide explains how to configure single sign-on." | Enterprise, compliance, training |

## Lines that work out loud

- Write for the ear: contractions, short words, active verbs. Read every line aloud at speaking pace.
- Put the important word at the end of the line, where stress naturally falls ("Review it every *Friday*.").
- Use signposts for steps ("One:", "Next,", "Last,") so the viewer can count along.
- Prefer concrete nouns and numbers you can show over adjectives ("in four minutes" beats "quickly").
- Avoid tongue-twisters and stacked sibilants; synthetic voices exaggerate them.

## Keywords

Choose the words a viewer would remember with the sound off: the number, the object, the action. Avoid filler ("really", "just") as keywords.

## CTA patterns

- Reference content: "Save this for your next launch."
- Series: "Follow for part two — the review ritual."
- Product: "Try it free for a week — link below." (only with a real, verified offer)

## Pronunciation notes

Flag brand names, acronyms and unusual words for voice-director (for example "Jotly — rhymes with 'hotly'"). Spell numbers the way they should be spoken when it matters ("twenty twenty-six", not "2026").
