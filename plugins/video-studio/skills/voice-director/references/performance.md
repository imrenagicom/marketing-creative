# Directing the performance

## From acting baseline to settings

| Baseline says | Try |
| --- | --- |
| warm, conversational, friend-like | `stability` ~0.4–0.5; occasional `[warm]` or `[chuckles]` |
| energetic creator, quick | `stability` ~0.3–0.4; tags like `[excited]` on the hook only |
| calm, authoritative, formal | `stability` ~0.6–0.7; no tags |
| deliberate pause before a reveal | end the previous line with "…" or put the reveal on its own line |

Tags steer delivery; too many make it theatrical. Use them where the baseline changes, not on every line.

## Casting

- Match age, energy and accent to the audience; for Indonesian, prefer native Indonesian voices over multilingual voices that sound translated.
- Audition with the hook and the CTA lines; they show range and warmth fastest.
- Keep one voice per character across the whole video and across language branches where possible.

## Judging a take

Listen once without the script, then once with it.

1. **Performance**: does it sound like the acting baseline, or like an announcer?
2. **Emphasis**: do the keywords carry stress? On-screen keywords that are swallowed in the audio feel mismatched.
3. **Names and terms**: brand names, numbers, English terms in Indonesian lines.
4. **Pace**: rushed lines, unnatural pauses, clipped first or last syllables.
5. **Consistency**: in `lines` mode, level and tone between lines.

Note issues per line id and retake only those lines (switch to `lines` mode if needed). Never speed up audio to fit a duration; cut words in the script instead.

## Indonesian specifics

- Particles (*kan*, *sih*, *dong*) carry tone; check they sound natural, not read.
- English terms often get Indonesian vowel sounds; check them and use a pronunciation dictionary when a term repeats.
- Numbers: confirm the voice reads them as the script intends (*dua ribu dua puluh enam* vs digits).
